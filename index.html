Where we are

Right now, campaigns run when a payment clears and pause until the next one. That keeps spending in line with cash flow, but it makes lead flow uneven, and roofing jobs usually go to whoever reaches the homeowner first. During a rush, leads wait longer for a call. During a quiet stretch, homeowners who search end up calling someone else.

Organic social and SEO keep working in between, but they can't carry the account alone. For the rest of this year and into next, we recommend steady campaign funding so the work can build momentum. At your own close rates, a steady 120 leads a month adds up to as much as $1.75M a year in roofing work.

001 / Why it matters
7×
More likely to connect when a lead is reached within the first hour

Businesses that reach a new lead within an hour are nearly seven times as likely to have a real conversation with the decision maker as those that wait even one hour longer. Roofing is tighter still, because homeowners often request several quotes at once and book the first contractor who calls back.

A steady daily flow lets your team call every lead within minutes. A burst creates a backlog, and a quiet stretch hands the next storm's homeowners to whoever is still advertising. It has already happened here: one qualified overnight lead booked with a competitor before the morning call.

Homeowners don't waitRoofing searches spike after storms. A homeowner who searches during a pause books with whoever shows up first, and that job is gone before campaigns come back on.

Paid campaigns restart coldAfter a pause, ad platforms go back into a learning period, so the first days back cost more and convert less while the campaign finds its audience again.
