The short version

Juice House leads have put 40 homeowners under contract or into claims with Roofing Pros, an estimated $260K to $700K in roofing work, with the first $83K already invoiced.

Right now, campaigns run when a payment clears and pause until the next one. That keeps spending in line with cash flow, but it makes lead flow uneven, and roofing jobs usually go to whoever reaches the homeowner first. During a rush, leads wait longer for a call. During a quiet stretch, homeowners who search end up calling someone else. Organic social and SEO keep working in between, but they can't carry the account alone.

For the rest of this year and into next, we recommend steady campaign funding so the work can build momentum. At your own close rates, a steady 120 leads a month is worth roughly $55K to $146K a month in roofing work.

On August 28 alone, 25 leads came in at once, more than a sales team can call back quickly. Then nothing for two weeks. The longest gap fell in what is historically the busiest stretch of hurricane season, when homeowners are most likely to be looking for a roofer.

What the 35 empty days could have produced: roughly $95K to $250K in roofing work. May, the last fully funded month, averaged about 6 leads a day. Across 35 days without a lead, that pace works out to about 210 leads. At Roofing Pros' own rates (2.6% signed, 7.0% including claims) and $17,405 per job, that's the range above. This is an estimate, not a forecast.

Speed to lead decides who gets the job

Businesses that reach a new lead within an hour are nearly seven times as likely to have a real conversation with the decision maker as those that wait even one hour longer. Roofing is tighter still, because homeowners often request several quotes at once and book the first contractor who calls back.

A steady daily flow lets your team call every lead within minutes. A burst creates a backlog, so leads sit while the team works through the pile. A quiet stretch is worse, because the next storm's homeowners find whoever is still advertising. It has already happened here: one qualified overnight lead booked with a competitor before the morning call.

Homeowners don't wait

Roofing searches spike after storms. A homeowner who searches during a pause books with whoever shows up first, and that job is gone before campaigns come back on.

Paid campaigns restart cold

After a pause, ad platforms go back into a learning period, so the first days back cost more and convert less while the campaign finds its audience again.
